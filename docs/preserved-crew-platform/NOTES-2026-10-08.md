## Grant access and reentry support (exploratory, not a requirement)

- A long-running motivation for this work is helping people who have difficulty finding support and navigating complicated processes: people returning from incarceration, people leaving rehabilitation, survivors of abuse or domestic violence rebuilding their lives, and older adults seeking retraining as work changes with AI.
- Grant funding may help these groups, but opportunities can be hard to find and applications are difficult to complete. In some settings, access is further complicated by relying on organizational grant writers who can be hard for applicants to reach.
- A possible future workflow would search public and private grants, match opportunities to a person's or organization's circumstances, explain eligibility and required evidence in plain language, help assemble and populate an application, then have an independent reviewer check requirements and completeness. The applicant or authorized organization would review and approve everything before submission.
- The aim is to make the process easier to navigate and improve access to support, not to promise eligibility, funding, or replace human judgment. This is a future vertical to explore, not a decision about the first product or MVP.

## Vertical portfolio direction (exploratory)

- Plan the product architecture with the full set of intended verticals in mind, so later workflows are not boxed out by assumptions made for the first one.
- Build and refine the verticals one at a time, giving each focused attention rather than developing all of them in parallel.
- Reserve real extension points in the shared core (for example, workflow definitions, role/model assignments, artifacts, review gates, and integrations), but avoid empty placeholder features or premature plugin machinery. Each vertical should become a usable, validated workflow when it is built.

## Conversational intake and user-owned continuity (exploratory)

- Intake should be a natural, adaptive interview, not require the user to arrive with a polished Markdown prompt. The conversation should draw out goals, context, constraints, and missing details, then produce useful structured artifacts (such as a spec, application, or story) for later stages.
- Treat the interview/conversation engine as a product capability in its own right. A possible creative use is character-perspective interviewing that stays grounded in the user's fictional canon and produces human, emotionally nuanced writing.
- Preserve continuity independently of any one model or provider: distinguish user-owned memories and preferences from the model/agent used in a session, and make that continuity inspectable, portable, and under user control.
- Aim for a supportive, nonjudgmental experience that is available when people need help outside ordinary business hours. Be candid about model fallibility; do not present the product as a substitute for professional or emergency care.
- These are product principles to explore across verticals, not commitments to a particular implementation or first release.

## Field-work visual reasoning example (exploratory)

- Construction example: for a garden/roundabout feature made from timber set into the ground, the desired exposed heights varied by roughly 1–2 inches, with no more than about 3 inches of difference between neighboring pieces. A schematic led a worker toward an unsuitable chainsaw cut; checking a site photo and the dimensions helped catch the issue, account for splintered material, and avoid unnecessary waste.
- Product implication: support image- and plan-grounded reasoning that combines site context, dimensions, layout, tolerances, and material condition. Show the measurements and assumptions used, flag conflicts or uncertain estimates, and require a person to verify the cut/layout before physical work. This is an illustrative case, not a validated accuracy claim or a committed feature.

## Cross-vertical architecture observations (exploratory recommendations)

- Preserve the engine's useful core: deterministic, code-owned phase sequencing; typed agent handoffs; explicit gates; bounded correction loops; and detailed run traces. Do not replace these with an unconstrained autonomous agent loop.
- Keep domain workflows as explicit, versioned implementations at first. Add workflow definition machinery only when a second real workflow demonstrates what should be configurable; avoid starting with a general DAG editor or plugin marketplace.
- Separate a target workspace (such as a repository or organization environment) from a domain case (such as one grant application or permit request). A case may collect its own interview, records, documents, decisions, and runs.
- Evolve artifacts beyond bare paths when the first non-software workflow requires it: track artifact type/version, source/provenance, status, and links to the phase that created or verified it. Requirements and extracted facts should retain evidence references and unknown/conflicting values.
- Make human decisions durable workflow gates that can pause and resume a run. Record the actor, time, action, and exact artifact version approved. A typed name alone is not authentication. Require explicit human approval for consequential external actions such as sending correspondence or submitting an application.
- Integrate external capabilities through narrow, allowlisted, auditable adapters (research sources, document/form handling, email), not broad shell access. Start draft-first for outbound messages and record action receipts/results.
- Keep workflow responsibility separate from model/provider selection. Configure the model, prompt, and allowed capabilities per specialist role so model choices can change without changing the domain workflow. The current engine is Pi-coupled in places; abstract that boundary only when another runtime/provider is actually needed.
- Suggested architecture proof before broad platform work: a fixture-based grant-application preparation workflow with synthetic applicant records and fixed source documents. Produce a cited requirements matrix, evidence-linked extracted facts, a draft, an independent completeness review, and a human approval of a specific version; stop before live submission. Test missing/conflicting evidence and traceability.
- These are recommendations derived from inspecting the Antigravity engine and both studios, not approved requirements or a commitment to start with grants.

## Document ingestion and retrieval (exploratory recommendation)

- Multiple verticals may need to ingest files, forms, plans, photos, email, or other records. Reserve a shared ingestion boundary now, separate from any one workflow: preserve the original, associate it with the right case, record type/source/version and access/retention rules, and produce extracted content with page/region references and provenance.
- Make search/retrieval return cited evidence linked to source locations, so specialist agents and reviewers can inspect where a claim came from. Keep the retrieval/index implementation replaceable and scoped to the appropriate case or organization.
- Do not build a full RAG stack, vector database, or generalized ingestion platform before a concrete workflow proves the needed formats, scale, privacy rules, and retrieval quality. Start with the smallest useful adapters and deterministic/lexical search where sufficient; add OCR, vision extraction, embeddings, or specialized indexes when validated by real cases.
- This is an architecture seam to preserve, not a commitment to build ingestion now.
